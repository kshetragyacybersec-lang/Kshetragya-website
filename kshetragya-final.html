क्षेत्रज्ञ Kshetragya · Sanskrit · क्षेत्र (Kshetra) + ज्ञ (Jna) "Knower of the Field"

Kshetra means the field — the domain, the terrain, the battlefield. Jna means the knower — the consciousness that perceives it completely. Together: the one whose knowledge of the field is total. In cybersecurity, that knowledge is your most powerful defence.

"Three thousand years ago, the Bhagavad Gita named the quality that wins every battle: complete knowledge of the field. We built a cybersecurity firm around that single idea — and carried it into the modern threat landscape."

क्षेत्रं क्षेत्री तथा कृत्स्नं प्रकाशयति भारत
"The field and its knower — together they illuminate everything, O Arjuna."
Bhagavad Gita · Chapter XIII · Verse 34

The Bhagavad Gita teaches that the Kshetragya — the knower of the field — possesses complete awareness of the terrain: what is present, what is absent, and what is hidden. This is not a metaphor we borrowed. It is the operating principle of every engagement we conduct. Before we defend your network, we know it more completely than those who would attack it. Every vulnerability, every misconfiguration, every hidden passage. That is Kshetragya.

ज्ञा Manual-first methodology

Automated scanners find the known. Our manual testing finds the logic flaws, chained misconfigurations, and attack paths that no tool can enumerate — the ones that become the actual breach.

सत् Compliance-grade deliverables

Every report is built for its real audience — your auditor, your compliance officer, or the enterprise customer demanding proof of testing before signing your contract.

धर् Domain-specialized ownership

Each partner owns one domain — network security, application security, or GRC. You engage a focused specialist, not a generalist reading about your problem for the first time.

ऋत Transparent, fixed engagements

Clear scope, clear deliverables, clear price before work begins. No retainer traps, no vague consulting hours, no surprises on your invoice.
